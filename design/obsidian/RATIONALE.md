# Obsidian Glass — why it looks the way it does

Five paragraphs. The spec is `SPEC.md`; the proof sheet is `mockups.html` (ten frames; the Model page from `design/model/BRIEF.md` and the Ledger drawn side by side so the fifth-tab decision can be made by eye).

**1. What obsidian is here, and why it is not navy.** Navy is the product's ink and its plate: it is what the gold diamond sits on and what every START chip is lettered in. Used as a ground for an hour-long session it does two things wrong on an OLED phone — it is bright enough that glass laid over it has nothing to refract, so a Liquid Glass bar over flat navy reads as a plain dark bar, and it makes the gold a warm accent on a blue field instead of the one lit thing on a black one. Obsidian is the navy's own hue (225°) pulled down to L\* 3.4: `#0A0C12`, near-black with a cool cast, not `#000` (true black smears when content scrolls on OLED and swallows every shadow). It keeps the identity in the DNA — the navy survives as the chip ink and under the mark — while giving the glass a floor of soft light to bend: a slow vertical ramp and two faint lights painted natively behind a transparent web view. Broadcast already owns the warm dark; this is the cool one, and the two are different rooms of the same house rather than a second brand.

**2. Why glass belongs on the controls and not on the content.** Apple's rule is explicit — Liquid Glass is "reserved for the navigation layer that floats above the content," "don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer," "always avoid glass on glass" — and the reason is the same one that decides our screens: the material exists to keep *controls* legible while *content* moves beneath them. A page of blurred cards inverts that. The moment the tab bar floats over a blurred card you have glass on glass, the material means nothing, and every card is a live backdrop re-sampled each frame on an A13. So the two bars and the one floating strip are real glass, and the cards are smoked glass instead: a 78% obsidian fill with a lit 1px top edge, no blur, no drop shadow. It costs nothing, it survives every accessibility setting unchanged, and it reads as depth because the ground's light passes through it. The scroll edge effect under the bars is not decoration either — cream text on bare glass directly over a gold START chip is 4.1:1 and fails; with the edge dim beneath it is 8.6:1. The mockup ships a checkbox that blurs the cards too, because the argument is easier to see than to read.

**3. How gold stays singular.** On paper gold is a warm chip; on obsidian it is the brightest object on the screen, which is exactly right for the one thing a row exists to say and wrong for anything else. So the budget is written down: gold *fills* are the START chips plus at most one primary verb per screen ("Set lineup on ESPN," "Open your week"); everything else that needs attention gets a 2px gold rule — the Needs-you card, the honesty banner, your column on the Trade Desk, the creator's ring — and the floating strip gets none, because it is already the float and a rule on glass samples as a gold-tinted bar; gold *text* is allowed only on numbers and times that carry a decision (`WK 1`, `Sun 1:00 PM ET`, the banner's date), because gold is 10.8:1 on obsidian and can carry a numeral where on paper it could not, and never on a word: kickers, `SMASH`, `VS`, `SPLIT`, `6 calls` and `wt 17` are cream or dim, since a word that is still true printed in grey should be grey. The first obsidian pass broke this in the content layer — the Lineup ran at six per cent saturated gold against under two on Home — and the fix was subtraction, not a new colour: the slot badges are plain dim mono, a neutral opponent has no outline at all and a smash opponent a cream one (the pips already say SMASH once), a locked player never gets a verdict fill, and a row's confidence sits beside the chip in dim mono rather than inside the gold. What is left is the chip, the verb, the rule, and the numbers that decide something. The tab bar is monochrome with the selection drawn as a white fill on the glass and only the glyph tinted, so the label stays legible; the bars themselves are never tinted — Apple's line, "when every element is tinted, nothing stands out," is the same rule the product already had.

**4. What the honesty banner looks like on glass.** It is the one element in the chrome that refuses the material. It docks inside the top bar, under the capsules, so it pushes content down and can never be scrolled away or floated over; it is opaque `wash-hot` with a 2px gold rule, cream text and the date in gold mono — "Couldn't refresh — showing week 1 as of Thu 10 Sep, 9:04 PM. Nothing in it has been re-checked since then." — with a 44pt Retry drawn as a gold outline so the gold-fill budget is untouched. A translucent warning over moving content is a quieter warning; on a screen where everything else is glass, the single solid warm strip is the loudest thing on it without being red. The league capsule above it repeats the fact in three words ("Saved copy · Thu 9:04 PM"), so the state is said twice, once on the glass and once in the strip, and Reduce Transparency changes nothing about either.

**5. Two things deliberately not done.** First, no blur on the content layer — not on cards, rows, decision cards, tables or the Trade Desk grid — even though the ask said "cards with backdrop-filter" and WebKit on iOS 26 can now sample native content through a transparent web view. Translucency without blur gives the whole visual effect; the blur would only add cost and a hierarchy problem, and it is blind to Reduce Transparency, which WebKit never exposed to CSS. Second, no second gold and no glow: no gold tint on the bars, no gold ring on feeds, no gold gradient, no outer glow on a pressed capsule or an active tab — pressed is a lift from within, attention is a rule. The obsidian version of "restraint" is that the screen has exactly one bright fill per verdict and one per verb, and the glass is felt at its edges rather than seen through.
